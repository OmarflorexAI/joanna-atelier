# Impeccable — Design Context

Persisted design context for the Johanna online catalog/portfolio.
Read this before any design work. Tokens live in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md);
scope and requirements live in [PRD.md](PRD.md).

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary — the prospective client.** Arrives from Johanna's Instagram bio link, almost
always on a phone, mid-scroll, knowing nothing about the site. They are deciding two
things in under ten seconds: *does she make the kind of thing I want*, and *is she the
real thing*. Their job-to-be-done is to see the work, believe the reputation, and reach
her without composing a DM from scratch.

**Secondary — press, stylists, collaborators.** Scanning for credibility signals and a
professional way to make contact.

Design consequence: the work must appear immediately. No splash screen, no long
scroll before the first piece, no interstitial about-me. Mobile at 390px is the
primary canvas, not an adaptation of the desktop view.

### Brand Personality

**Refined · Warm · Exclusive.**

Luxury-adjacent but unmistakably human. She hand-makes custom pieces for artists and
celebrities in the Dominican Republic — the site must carry that stature without
becoming cold, corporate, or sterile. Warmth is what separates her from a fashion
house's website; refinement is what separates her from a craft marketplace.

Voice: confident and spare. She does not oversell — the work and the client list do
that. Copy is short, declarative, first-person where it's her speaking. Never breathless,
never "passionate about," never exclamation marks.

Emotional goal: a visitor should feel they have found someone, not been marketed to.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Editorial gallery.** The site behaves like a well-made fashion book: generous margins,
strong typographic scale contrast, imagery given room to be looked at. The interface
recedes so the garments carry all the color in the frame.

**Theme: light mode only.** A warm-neutral base — bone and oat surfaces, near-black
brown ink, taupe secondary text — with **one** jewel accent reserved almost entirely
for calls to action, focus rings, and the rare emphatic mark. The accent must never
compete with a photograph.

**Reference — [lannino.com](https://lannino.com/).** We borrow *structure*, never look:

- Its index-style navigation — a short, plainly-labelled route list rather than a
  conventional menu bar. (The `01/02/03` numbering was tried and **removed at client
  request**: it read as decorative clutter on a garment catalog.)
- Oversized statement typography used as content, not decoration.
- The oversized closing CTA rendered at display scale rather than as a small button.
- Generous vertical rhythm (112px+) between sections.

**Deliberately rejected from the reference:**

- Its cream/ember palette — explicitly ruled out by the client.
- Its browser-chrome card headers. Those are a developer's affordance for showing
  websites; wrapping a photograph of a garment in a fake browser bar would be absurd.
- Its hierarchy. Lannino sells *services*, so the site is about him and the work is
  evidence. Johanna sells *objects*, so the site is about the pieces and she is the
  evidence. The work leads; her story arrives second.

**Anti-references — what this must never resemble:** a template marketplace theme, a
Shopify storefront, a startup landing page, or anything with a testimonial carousel.
No dark mode with glowing accents. No glassmorphism. No purple-blue gradients. No
identical card grids. No large rounded icons above headings. If it reads as
AI-generated, it has failed.

### Medium

Her pieces are **fashion — custom garments and wearables.** This is a hard constraint
on layout:

- Portrait **3:4** is the primary crop; full-body shots need vertical room.
- Detail macro shots (fabric, stitching, beadwork) carry the hand-made claim and must
  be first-class in the layout, not thumbnails.
- On-body and event imagery is the proof of stature — plan for it.
- The grid must never crop a garment's silhouette to fit a uniform box.

### Design Principles

1. **The garment is the only hero.** Every interface decision recedes in favor of the
   photograph. If a design element competes with a piece for attention, the element loses.

2. **Prove the craft, never claim it.** "Hand-made" is worthless as a word and priceless
   as an image. Macro texture, process, materials, and time — show these instead of
   writing adjectives.

3. **Restraint reads as expensive.** One accent color. Two surfaces per view. Motion only
   where it clarifies. The absence of decoration is the luxury signal.

4. **Answer the question before it's asked.** Every repeat DM she receives — lead time,
   process, pricing, shipping — is a design failure to be solved with a linkable
   anchor. The site's job is to make the DM unnecessary.

5. **Contact must feel like an invitation, not a form.** The single highest-value
   interaction on the site. It gets the most craft: underline-only fields, considered
   transitions, and a submit state that feels like a small reward.

## Implementation Notes

- **Palette direction:** warm neutrals + one jewel accent (bone/oat base, near-black
  brown ink, taupe muted, a single deep jewel tone for action). Exact values to be
  fixed in `CLAUDE.md` when the visual build begins — the Lannino token table there is
  superseded for color, while its *spacing, radius, and motion* scales remain sound
  and are retained.
- **Typography:** keep a distinctive display/body pairing with strong scale contrast.
  No Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, or system defaults. Monospace, if used at all, only for
  genuine micro-labels — never as shorthand for "technical."
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA for all content text. Visible focus rings in the accent.
  All motion gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Errors carry text, never
  color alone.
